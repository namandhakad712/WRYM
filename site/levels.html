Legend · Plate IV

The pieces, drawn as they are meant

Reading the engraving

The earthworm — ringed, blunt-snouted, bead-eyed — grows by one segment per apple and may split, leaving inert remains that still carry weight. Dirt is dug through at the cost of a turn. Crates push sokoban-style and park on pressure plates; a door stays sealed while any plate stands unweighted. One-way valves only forgive travel with the arrow. Ice slides the worm until something stops it — one action, many cells. Bridges crumble the instant the last weight leaves them. Keys open locks permanently. Spikes kill on contact, even mid-slide. The burrow — the dark hole in the earth — opens only when every apple is eaten.
